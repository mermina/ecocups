ESG (Environmental, Social, Governance) and CSR (Corporate Social Responsibility) reporting is now a normal part of doing business in Iceland — expected by partners, clients, investors and, increasingly, public-sector buyers. The case for it is simple: small, documented choices add up, and the cups your team and customers use every day are a visible, easy win you can point to.

In Iceland, Festa — the Icelandic Centre for Sustainability brings together close to 200 organisations, from the country's largest companies to small businesses, the City of Reykjavík and universities, to advance sustainability and the UN Sustainable Development Goals. Festa also runs a free carbon tool, ClimatePulse. Switching to certified compostable cups is exactly the kind of concrete, reportable step that fits naturally into an ESG or CSR story.

How this maps to your reporting

  • GRI 306 (Waste): the kg / tonnes of waste diverted from disposal.
  • ESRS E5 — resource use & circular economy (EU CSRD): the shift from ~0% to 100% compostable & recyclable.
  • Carbon (recyclability): recycling cuts a paper cup's footprint ~54% (VTT/Huhtamaki); EcoCups can be recycled, PE/PLA can't. We don't publish a Scope 3 CO₂ figure until certified supplier data is available.

Maps to GRI 306 (Waste) · ESRS E5 / EU CSRD (resource use & circular economy) — an alignment guide showing where each figure fits in these standards, not a claim of certified or audited compliance.
How these figures are calculated: Cups/year = people × ~2 cups per workday × days open. Material kept from landfill/incineration = cups/year × ~6.9 g per cup (8 oz cup), shown in kg/tonnes (the ESG/GRI waste unit); EcoCups count as recovered (compostable / paper-recyclable), not landfilled. Plastic is shown as a status, not a weight: PE- and PLA-lined cups both carry a plastic lining (PLA counts as plastic under EU rules), while EcoCups are certified ≥98% plastic-free (Flustix FPPC036). Carbon: recycling cuts a paper cup's footprint by ~54% (VTT/Huhtamaki) and EcoCups are recyclable while PE/PLA aren't — so the win is recyclability; we don't publish a single CO₂ figure, pending certified supplier data. These figures compare waste and material end-of-life only — not full lifecycle or production impacts.
